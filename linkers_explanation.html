Using Linkers

Linkers connect ideas: reason, result, purpose, contrast and comparison. Choosing the right meaning is only half the job — at C1, the key question is what comes after the linker: a full clause, a noun, an infinitive, or a new sentence.

The key question: what comes after?

Linkers with similar meanings often need different grammar. Although and despite both express contrast, but although is followed by a clause (subject + verb) and despite by a noun or an -ing form.
+ clause Although he works long hours, he doesn't earn much money.
+ noun I danced all night despite my uncomfortable shoes.
+ sentence The food was delicious. However, it was far too expensive.
+ to + verb I got up early in order to clean the apartment.

Linkers by meaning

Linker Followed by Example
Reason: because / as / since clause We ate the cheese because we were hungry.
Reason: because of / due to noun The party was canceled due to bad weather.
Result: so clause (same sentence) I was tired, so I went to bed.
Result: As a result / Consequently / Therefore new sentence + comma The movie was boring. As a result, we left early.
Result: or (= if not) clause Please hurry up or we'll miss the train.
Purpose: to / in order to / so as to base verb I got up early so as to clean the apartment.
Purpose: so (that) clause (often with can / could) I joined a gym so that I could get in shape.
Contrast: although / even though clause Even though it rained, we had fun.
Contrast: despite / in spite of noun / -ing / the fact that + clause In spite of being tired, I went out.
Contrast: However / Nevertheless new sentence + comma It was cheap. However, it was ugly.
Comparison: whereas / while clause I walk to college, whereas my friends take the subway.
Comparison: unlike noun Unlike my sister, I'm tall, with dark hair.

Clause or noun? Same idea, different linker

Many linkers come in pairs: one for a clause, one for a noun / -ing. When you change the linker, you must change the grammar after it.

+ clause (subject + verb)

because the weather was bad

although I was very tired

whereas my sister is short

+ noun / -ing

because of / due to the bad weather

despite / in spite of being very tired

unlike my sister

⚠ Need a clause after despite? Add the fact that

Despite and in spite of can't take a clause directly — but the fact that turns the clause into a noun phrase:

  • ❌ Eloy is loved by his students despite he is very strict.
  • ✅ …despite the fact that he is very strict.  ·  ✅ …despite being very strict.

However, Therefore, Consequently… — punctuation matters

These linkers connect two separate sentences. Use a full stop (or a semicolon) before them and a comma after them. A comma alone before however is not enough.

Full stop + linker + comma Our sales have doubled. However, we are still not making a profit.
Semicolon + linker + comma We're expanding our company; consequently, we're advertising for new staff.
⚠ As a result vs as a result of
  • The roads were icy. As a result, schools closed. (new sentence)
  • As a result of the icy roads, schools closed. (+ noun)

Purpose: in order to, so as to, so that

to / in order to / so as to + base verb We've made some changes to the menu in order to keep costs down.
Negative: in order not to / so as not to I crept quietly out of the room so as not to disturb her.
so (that) + subject + can / could / will You need to leave now so that you get there before dinner.
⚠ Where does not go?

Not goes before to: so as not to, in order not to — not so as to not.

❌ Common errors

1. Despite / in spite of + clause
❌ In spite of I was very tired, I enjoyed our dinner out.
✅ In spite of being very tired… / In spite of the fact that I was very tired…
2. As a result + noun (missing of)
❌ As a result the shortage of eggs, we're not serving pancakes.
✅ As a result of the shortage of eggs, we're not serving pancakes.
3. A comma before however / nevertheless
❌ Our sales have doubled, however we are still not making a profit.
✅ Our sales have doubled. However, we are still not making a profit.
4. Because of / due to + clause
❌ She took time off work due to she was ill.
✅ She took time off work because she was ill. / …due to illness.
5. Whereas + noun / unlike + clause
❌ Whereas you, I don't enjoy working out.
✅ Unlike you, I don't enjoy working out.  ·  ✅ Whereas you love it, I don't enjoy working out.

💡 Step-by-step: choosing a linker

1
What's the relationship?
reason · result · purpose · contrast · comparison
2
Look at what comes next.
Subject + verb → because, although, whereas, so that
Noun / -ing → because of, due to, despite, unlike
Base verb → to, in order to, so as to
3
Is it a new sentence?
Use However, Nevertheless, Therefore, Consequently, As a result — full stop before, comma after.
4
Changing the linker? Change the grammar.
although I was tired → despite being tired / despite the fact that I was tired
5
Check the punctuation and not.
Comma after sentence linkers · so as not to, not so as to not
