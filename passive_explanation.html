The Passive: be / get, causatives, -ing & infinitives

We use the passive when the action matters more than the person who does it. At C1, the challenge is choosing the right version: be or get, a causative with have or get, and passive forms after other verbs — being told or to be told.

Why use the passive?

The passive puts the focus on what happens (or what happened to someone), not on who does it. The person who does the action is often unknown, obvious or unimportant. If we want to mention them, we use by.
Active Someone reported the incident to the police.
Passive The incident was reported to the police.

The basic form is always be (or get) + past participle. The tense is carried by be / get; the main verb is always the participle (reported, broken, told).

be passive vs get passive

be passive — neutral, works everywhere

The standard passive. Use it in formal and informal English, in writing and speech, with any verb.

be Plans for a new community center were announced yesterday.
be Rania has been promoted twice since she joined.
get passive — informal, things that happen

More informal and typical of speech. It describes something that happens to someone or something — often unexpected, accidental or negative.

get Our house got broken into last week.
get Guess what I just got given? Two free concert tickets!
⚠ get needs an action

Get describes a change or an event, so it doesn't work with state verbs like love, know, respect, own, believe. Use be.

  • ❌ He got loved by all his coworkers.
  • ✅ He was loved by all his coworkers.

The passive in different tenses

Tense be passive get passive
Present simple It is cleaned every day. It gets cleaned every day.
Past simple He was fired. He got fired.
Continuous The toilets were being cleaned. The toilets were getting cleaned.
Present perfect She has been promoted. She has got(ten) promoted.
Future / modals You will be told soon. You might get told off.

Causative: have / get something done

We use the causative when someone else does something for us — usually a service we ask or pay for. The order is fixed: have / get + object + past participle.

Active I fixed my car. (I did it myself)
Causative I had my car fixed. (a mechanic did it)
have — neutral I need to have this parcel delivered.
get — more informal She recently got her eyes tested.
Something bad that happened to you I had my phone stolen on the train.

being done or to be done?

After some verbs, adjectives and prepositions, the passive appears as an -ing form or an infinitive. The word before it decides which one — the same rule as in the active (I hate waiting / I hope to win).

being + past participle

After hate, mind, remember, enjoy, avoid, after prepositions (of, about, without), and as the subject of a sentence.

I hate being made to feel left out.

I dream of being offered an amazing house.

Being ordered around is not something I enjoy.

to be / to get + past participle

After hope, want, expect, need, wait, and after adjectives like delighted, likely, happy.

I'm delighted to be invited to join the group.

That building needs to get repainted.

He's likely to be called for an interview.

⚠ Same verb, different form

Compare these two sentences — the word before the passive decides:

  • I remember the shock of being told the news. (preposition of → -ing)
  • Are you still waiting to be told the results? (wait → infinitive)

❌ Common errors

1. Using the past simple instead of the past participle
❌ A window somehow got broke.  ·  ❌ My bike was stole.
✅ A window somehow got broken.  ·  ✅ My bike was stolen.
2. Wrong word order in the causative
❌ I had fixed my car yesterday. (= past perfect: I fixed it myself)
✅ I had my car fixed yesterday. (object before the participle)
3. Using get with a state verb
❌ She got respected by everyone.
✅ She was respected by everyone.
4. Mixing up being and to be
❌ I hope being included on the list.  ·  ❌ Do you mind to be picked up later?
✅ I hope to be included on the list.  ·  ✅ Do you mind being picked up later?
5. Forgetting be / get
❌ The incident reported to the police.
✅ The incident was reported to the police.

💡 Step-by-step: which passive?

1
Did someone else do it for you (a service)?
Yes → causative: have / get + object + participle → I had my eyes tested.
2
Is it formal, or is the verb a state verb?
Yes → be passive  |  Informal event, often unexpected → get is possible
3
Does the passive come after another verb, an adjective or a preposition?
hate, mind, remember, prepositions → being + participle
hope, need, wait, likely, delighted → to be + participle
4
Put the tense on be / get.
was reported, has been promoted, were being cleaned, got fired
5
Check the past participle.
broken (not broke), stolen (not stole), given, told, written
