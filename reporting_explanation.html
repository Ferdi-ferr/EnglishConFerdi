Verb Patterns: Reporting

When we report what someone said, asked or ordered, the reporting verb decides the pattern that follows: that + clause, if / wh- + statement order, object + to-infinitive, or that + subject + base form.

Four basic patterns

What was said Pattern Example
Statement say / tell sb + (that) + clause They said (that) the internet was down.
wh- question ask / wonder + wh- word + statement order I wondered what life without electricity was like.
yes/no question ask (sb) + if / whether + statement order They asked me if they should catch an earlier train.
Command / request tell / ask / order / warn + object + (not) to + verb He ordered us to put down our devices.

Backshift: one step into the past

When the reporting verb is in the past (said, asked, told), the verb in the reported part usually moves one step back in time. Pronouns, time and place words change too.

Direct speech Reported speech Example
present simplepast simple“It is down.” → they said it was down
present continuouspast continuous“I'm coming.” → she said she was coming
past simple / present perfectpast perfect“I've lost my keys.” → she said she had lost her keys
will / can / maywould / could / might“We'll call.” → they said they would call
tomorrow / yesterdaythe next day / the day before“…tomorrow.” → …the next day
this / here / nowthat / there / then“I like this place.” → he said he liked that place

Reported questions: statement word order

A reported question is not a question any more: use subject + verb order, no do / does / did, and no question mark.

Direct “Where do you live?” she asked me.
Reported She asked me where I lived.
Direct “Are you coming to the party?” Leo asked.
Reported Leo asked me if / whether I was coming to the party.

say vs tell, and reporting commands

say — no person after it

She said (that) she was tired.

She said to me (that) she was tired.

tell — always tell somebody

She told me (that) she was tired.

He told us to wait outside.

Command → object + to + verb “Put down your devices!” → He ordered us to put down our devices.
Negative command → object + not to + verb “Please don't be late.” → The teacher asked the students not to be late.

Reporting in the present: no backshift

When the reporting verb is in the present (says, tells, often tells) — for something said regularly or still true now — the tenses don't change.

Present reporting verb → same tense My teacher says my progress is good.
Habit / still true My friends often tell me about the latest technology they want.

The subjunctive after demand, suggest, insist, recommend

After verbs like demand, suggest, insist, recommend, request + that, we use the base form of the verb for every subject — no -s, no past, no backshift. The negative is not + base form.

Subjunctive I demanded that they give me a pay raise.
Subjunctive We suggested that he try a different smartwatch. (not tries / tried)
Negative The doctor recommended that she not drink coffee.
⚠ suggest doesn't take an object + to
  • ❌ We suggested him to try a different smartwatch.
  • ✅ We suggested (that) he try a different smartwatch.

❌ Common errors

1. Question word order in a reported question
❌ She asked me where did I live.
✅ She asked me where I lived.
2. say + person
❌ He said me that he was busy.
✅ He told me that he was busy.  ·  ✅ He said that he was busy.
3. Forgetting to backshift after a past reporting verb
❌ Ana told me she has lost her keys.
✅ Ana told me she had lost her keys.
4. that or a full clause after tell / ask for commands
❌ The guard told the children don't touch the paintings.
✅ The guard told the children not to touch the paintings.
5. Adding -s or backshifting in the subjunctive
❌ I demanded that he gives / gave me a refund.
✅ I demanded that he give me a refund.
